Stock Selection Methodology

Selection Framework — 4 Steps

1

Universe: NIFTY 50

Started with 50 constituent stocks. Removed stocks with insufficient history (< 2010), yielding 47 eligible stocks.

2

Return Score (Composite, percentile-ranked)

RetScore = 40% × CAGR + 30% × Excess Return vs NIFTY + 30% × Sharpe Ratio
Each component ranked 0–100 percentile across all 47 stocks.

3

Risk Score (Composite, percentile-ranked)

RiskScore = 25% × Volatility + 20% × Beta + 20% × VaR₉₅ + 25% × ES₉₅ + 10% × MaxDrawdown

4

4-Quadrant Classification & Selection

Median-split → 4 quadrants. Selected 10 stocks each from High Risk · High Return (Port A) and Low Risk · High Return (Port B). Max 2 stocks per sector for diversification.

Weighting Rationale

Portfolio A — CAGR-Rank Weighting

Stocks ranked by CAGR; highest CAGR receives highest weight. Minimum floor of 4% per stock. BEL (CAGR rank #1 at time of construction) received 30%. Reflects a return-maximisation philosophy.

Portfolio B — Risk-Score Weighting

Stocks shortlisted from the Low Risk–High Return quadrant were ranked based on their risk scores, with greater allocation preference given to stocks exhibiting lower risk. The weighting methodology aims to balance return potential with downside risk, avoiding excessive concentration in high-risk stocks.

Assumption: All weights are applied to the full period data. Weights are frozen at construction — no rebalancing is modelled. This is a back-test, not a live strategy.

Data Sources & Limitations

  • Price data: Yahoo Finance via NSE ticker suffixes (.NS)
  • Risk-free rate: 6% p.a. (approximate Indian repo rate)
  • VaR/ES: Both historical simulation & parametric (normal) methods used
  • GARCH model not applied — vol clustering not captured
  • Monte Carlo VaR not included — normal distribution assumed for parametric
  • Correlation matrix computed on full-period daily returns
  • Covariance stationarity assumed (may not hold across regimes)

Key Findings & Summary

1. Risk Labels Hold Under Stress ✅
Portfolio A exceeds Portfolio B's VaR, ES, volatility, and drawdown across all three time periods. The classification is not overfitted to the full period — it's regime-robust.
2. Crisis Amplifies Risk Asymmetrically ✅
Portfolio A's volatility nearly doubles in the COVID crisis (22.3%→31.1%). Portfolio B's rise is more modest (+44%). This confirms that "high-risk" stocks suffer disproportionately during tail events.
3. ES Consistently Worse Than VaR ✅
In every scenario, ES (CVaR) reveals a deeper tail than VaR suggests. Crisis PA: VaR 99% = −5.47% but ES 99% = −7.60% — on the worst 1% of days, average loss is 39% worse than VaR implies. This is the core regulatory justification for shifting from VaR to ES (Basel III).
4. Intuitive Weights ≠ Optimal Weights ⚠️
Portfolio A's CAGR-rank weighting underperforms Max-Sharpe by ≈6.6% annual return at the same risk level. Intuitive metrics (CAGR rank) do not capture risk-adjusted return optimization.
5. Historical vs Parametric VaR Diverge in Crises ⚠️
During calm periods, both methods agree closely. During crisis, Parametric VaR is less severe than Historical VaR — because the normal distribution assumption understates fat tails when actual return distributions become highly leptokurtic.
6. Portfolio B Dominates on Risk-Adjusted Basis 🏆
Portfolio B delivers more return (21.5% vs 18.6%), far less risk (13.0% vs 22.3%), better Sharpe (1.04 vs 0.50), smaller drawdown (24.5% vs 26.12%), and lower VaR/ES. By every risk-adjusted metric, Portfolio B is the superior allocation.

Final Risk-Return Summary

Limitations & Future Work

  • No GARCH modelling — volatility clustering and regime changes not captured
  • No Monte Carlo VaR — parametric assumes normality; actual returns are fat-tailed
  • Weights are frozen — no rebalancing modelled; real portfolios drift over time
  • Covariance matrix uses full-period data — rolling-window EWMA would be more accurate
  • Beta computed vs own portfolio return (not NIFTY) — cannot directly compare to market beta
  • Efficient frontier is approximated — full Lagrangian-constrained optimization with scipy would be more precise
  • Sector concentration in Portfolio A (2 Adani group stocks) increases idiosyncratic risk
